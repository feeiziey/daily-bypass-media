**Full script**
Rome is farther north than New York. New York sits at 40.7 degrees north. Rome, at almost 42. Follow New York's latitude east, and it runs straight into Madrid. It keeps going. Paris is north of Seattle. London is north of Calgary. Venice and Montreal share the same line. Even Canada's southernmost point, a tiny island in Lake Erie, is south of Rome. Yet in January, New York averages about 1 degree Celsius. Rome, almost 8. Most people credit the Gulf Stream. But a 2002 study found the bigger reason is the wind. Westerlies blow across an ocean that stored up summer heat, and carry it into Europe. New York's winter winds come from a frozen continent. So Rome isn't warm because it's south. It's warm because it's downwind.
