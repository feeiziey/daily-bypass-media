**Full script**
The Amazon rainforest is fertilized by the Sahara Desert. The fertilizer is dust. Some of it starts here: the Bodélé Depression in Chad, the dustiest place on Earth. Around 7,000 years ago, this was a lake bigger than Germany. When it dried, it left a floor of fossil algae, rich in phosphorus. Winds lift it into the sky, and carry it west across the Atlantic. On average, 182 million tons of dust leave the Sahara every year. 132 million are still in the air off South America. And 27.7 million tons fall on the Amazon. Hidden inside: about 22,000 tons of phosphorus. Roughly what the rainforest loses every year, washed away by rain and floods. The jungle's soil is poor. The desert tops it up. The largest hot desert on Earth, feeding the largest rainforest.
