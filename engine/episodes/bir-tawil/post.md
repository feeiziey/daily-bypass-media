This desert is the size of Mauritius, and no country on Earth wants it. It's called Bir Tawil, between Egypt and Sudan. In 1899, the border here was a straight line: the 22nd parallel. In 1902, Britain drew a second line, based on how local tribes used the land. It dipped south around Bir Tawil, and bulged north along the coast. Egypt claims the first line. That gives it this coastal triangle: Halaib. Sudan claims the second. That gives Halaib to Sudan. Here's the catch. Egypt's line puts Bir Tawil in Sudan. Sudan's line puts it in Egypt. Claim Bir Tawil, and you accept the other line. You lose Halaib. Bir Tawil is about 2,060 square kilometers of rock and sand. Halaib is ten times bigger, with a Red Sea coast. So Egypt and Sudan agree on just one thing: Bir Tawil belongs to the other one.
