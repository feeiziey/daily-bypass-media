**Full script**
China is about five time zones wide. It runs on one clock. From the Pamir Mountains to the Amur River, it spans over 60 degrees of longitude. The sun needs about four hours to cross it. But every clock reads Beijing Time. UTC+8. Until 1949, China had five time zones. Then the new government made it one. Now watch the sun move. The clocks don't care. Out west in Kashgar, solar noon arrives around 3 PM. In winter, sunrise comes around 10 AM. In summer, sunset is after 10 PM. Kashgar's clock runs about 3 hours ahead of its sun. So many Uyghurs keep their own, unofficial clock. Xinjiang Time. Two hours behind Beijing. And at the Afghan border, in the Wakhan Corridor, the clock jumps 3½ hours. The biggest clock jump across any land border on Earth. One country. One clock. And the sun still keeps five.
